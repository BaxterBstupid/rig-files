# LiDAR–Camera Capture Rig Post-Mortem: RealityScan Timing Exports and Diagnosing Camera–LiDAR Time Sync (τ) in ROS2

## TL;DR
- **RealityScan 2.x does not store, expose, or export any per-image capture timestamp** — not from EXIF DateTimeOriginal, not in XMP, not in the registration/CSV exports, and not in any report variable. For PNGs extracted from a ROS2 bag (which normally carry no EXIF), RealityScan has no timing information at all; your camera–LiDAR temporal offset (τ) must be reckoned entirely from the ROS2 bag's own timestamps, not from anything RealityScan produces.
- **The robust way to measure τ is motion-based cross-correlation:** wave the rig with jerky rotation, extract an angular-velocity time series from the LiDAR-inertial side (Point-LIO odometry or the raw `/unilidar/imu` gyro) and a matching rotation signal from the camera (optical flow or essential-matrix rotation between consecutive frames), then find the time-shift that maximizes the correlation between the two signals — that shift is τ. Reading `header.stamp` differences alone is a useful first-order check but will not give you true sync.
- **Timestamps alone are insufficient because a ROS `header.stamp` records when the driver created the message, not when photons/points were captured.** A USB (UVC) camera adds tens to a few hundred milliseconds of pipeline/exposure/transfer latency that never appears in the stamp, so your prior ~150–200 ms estimate is entirely plausible and is a latency you must *measure*, not read. RealityScan's outputs still add value to the post-mortem — solved poses, per-camera reprojection error, component structure, and tie-point tracks let you cross-check whether motion blur or a bad τ corrupted alignment.

## Key Findings

**1. RealityScan timestamp export — a definitive "no."** Epic's RealityScan Help documents every variable available in its report engine and its exports. The Images scope (`ExportImagePriors`, `IterateImages`, `GetImageInfo`) and the Cameras scope (`ExportCamera`/`ExportCameras`, `CameraErrors`) expose only geometry, coordinate, calibration, lens-distortion, and error fields — there is no date/time or acquisition-timestamp variable anywhere. The XMP schema (`capturingreality.com/ns/xcr/1.1#`) carries pose, rotation, distortion, focal length, principal point, rig, and texturing/meshing flags — no time attribute. RealityScan reads EXIF only for resolution, 35 mm-equivalent focal length, sensor size, make/model, and GPS; the "Cache image metadata" feature saves a `crmeta.db` "with metadata from EXIF (such as resolution)" and nothing about capture time. Conclusion: timing must be tracked externally (e.g., bake the ROS timestamp into the filename or keep a filename→ros_time manifest). (RealityCapture was rebranded to RealityScan by Epic in June 2025; the current documented line is 2.1/2.1.1 and 2.2, the latter adding AMD RDNA 3/4 GPU support — none of these releases introduces a time field.)

**2. What RealityScan DOES export for a post-mortem.** Even without timing, RealityScan is a strong diagnostic tool for whether a capture *fused* well:
- **Registration export** (ALIGNMENT ▸ Export ▸ Registration): "Internal/External camera parameters" writes a CSV of solved intrinsics + extrinsics per camera; XMP export ("RealityScan XMP") writes per-image pose/calibration sidecars; also Bundler/COLMAP formats (in 2.1.1 the COLMAP exporter supports flat or standard directory layout, binary/ASCII, and auto-filters low-accuracy points).
- **Sparse tie-point cloud** (Export ▸ Point Cloud ▸ "Sparse point cloud" as PLY).
- **Reports** (Registration and Georeferencing Accuracy Report; Selected Component Report; Selected Model Report), plus fully scriptable custom report templates.
- **Per-camera diagnostics** via `CameraErrors`: reprojection error distribution (min/max/mean/median/stdev/mode), tie-point count, image coverage %, and a "tracking error" distribution (how many other cameras see each tie point). Component stats give cameras aligned, total projections, average track length, and mean/median/max reprojection error. `ExportMisalignmentCameras` reports weak inter-camera connection strengths.
- **Control-point / GCP residuals** exportable via ALIGNMENT ▸ Export ▸ Control Points / Ground Control, including computed ("actual") vs. prior positions.

**3–4. Timestamps vs. true capture time.** In ROS2 there are three distinct times: (a) the **bag receive/record time** (wall-clock arrival at the recorder, corrupted by network/USB jitter and retransmission — a ROS Answers thread documents per-message arrival delays of 119–570 ms and even out-of-order messages, with the advice to "use some 'source timestamp' for sensor messages if it is possible"); (b) the **`header.stamp`** (set by the driver, ideally at start of exposure/scan but for a UVC camera usually set at buffer-dequeue, *after* exposure+USB transfer+decode); and (c) the **true capture time** of the photons/points, which no field directly records. The gap between (b) and (c) is the sensor latency you are hunting. The Unitree L2 point cloud carries a per-point `time` field — the official `unilidar_sdk2` example prints `first 10 points (x,y,z,intensity,time,ring)` with `ringNum = 18` — so LiDAR points can be de-skewed to their true sub-scan sampling instants. Point-LIO requires this: its README warns that "The warning message 'Failed to find match for field time.' means the timestamps of each LiDAR points are missed in the rosbag file. That is important because Point-LIO processes at the sampling time of each LiDAR point." The camera has no equivalent — a single stamp per frame — which is exactly why an unsynchronized USB camera is the weak link.

**5. Motion-based temporal calibration (the robust method).** The standard, hardware-free technique is cross-correlation of a shared 1-D motion signal (usually angular-velocity magnitude): τ* = argmax over δt of Σ ζ_a(t+δt)·ζ_b(t). Move the rig with rich, jerky rotation; get one angular-velocity trace from the LiDAR-inertial side and one from the camera (optical flow, or rotation recovered from the essential matrix / homography between consecutive frames), resample both to a common high rate, and slide one against the other. Jerky, multi-axis, feature-dense motion works; slow smooth pans, sparse/low-texture scenes, and single-axis motion fail (degenerate/unobservable — one DoF of 3-D orientation can be unobservable). The Mair et al. cross-correlation approach (the lineage cited by hku-mars' LI-Init) is "reliable for small delays but yields incorrect optima for large delays"; a companion phase-congruency method achieved 19.3 ms mean error versus 360 ms for plain cross-correlation in a long-delay experiment, and Qiu's trace-correlation-plus-quadratic-fit refinement reaches sub-0.5 ms. So: cross-correlate for the coarse peak, then refine with quadratic-peak interpolation or phase-congruency/CCA.

**6. Off-the-shelf tooling.** There is no single turnkey "ROS2 camera-LiDAR temporal calibration" package that outputs τ directly for an arbitrary rig; temporal calibration is usually folded into camera-IMU or LiDAR-IMU tools:
- **Kalibr** (ethz-asl/kalibr, Furgale et al.) does spatiotemporal camera-IMU calibration via `kalibr_calibrate_imu_camera --time-calibration`; it embeds the temporal offset as a B-spline optimization variable and seeds it from "correlating absolute angular velocities as perceived independently by camera and IMU," outputting a `time_offset`.
- **LI-Init** (hku-mars/LiDAR_IMU_Init, IROS 2022) is "a robust, real-time initialization method [that] calibrates the temporal offset and extrinsic parameter between LiDARs and IMUs… does not require any target or extra sensor," using a "zero-centered cross correlation" of angular velocity.
- **direct_visual_lidar_calibration** (koide3, ICRA 2023) is the best-known "target-less LiDAR-camera calibration [ROS1/ROS2]" package (MIT license) — but it solves *spatial* extrinsics only and assumes the sensors are already time-synced (it requires the rig be at rest at the start of each bag so the first cloud and image coincide).

For camera-LiDAR *time* offset specifically, roll your own cross-correlation script or use the IMU as the bridge: camera↔IMU via Kalibr, LiDAR↔IMU via LI-Init, then compose.

## Details

### RealityScan (Epic Games) — timing exports and diagnostics

RealityScan is the current (2025–2026) name for RealityCapture. Releases 2.1.1 and 2.2 are documented on Epic's developer site, and CLI verbs were renamed — per Epic's 2.1.1 notes, "loadColmap replaces the importColmap command. loadBundler replaces the importBundler command. importTrajectory replaces the importFlightLog command."

**No capture timestamp, confirmed against the report-engine variable lists.** The report system's Images scope and Cameras scope enumerate every field the engine can emit. `ExportImagePriors` lists position, orientation, coordinate-system, INS-offset, calibration-group, and lens-distortion priors (inputX/Y/Z, inputLat/Lon/Alt, inputYaw/Pitch/Roll, inputOmega/Phi/Kappa, inputF, inputPX/PY, inputK1–K4, inputT1/T2, etc.) — no time field. `ExportCameras` similarly emits index, image path/name, solved x/y/z, yaw/pitch/roll, omega/phi/kappa, focal length, principal point, distortion coefficients, rotation matrices, and prior-position errors — no timestamp. The XMP export schema and real exported XMP files contain only pose/calibration/rig/texturing tags (`xcr:PosePrior`, `xcr:Rotation`, `xcr:DistortionModel`, `xcr:FocalLength35mm`, `xcr:PrincipalPointU/V`, `xcr:Rig`, `xcr:InTexturing`, `xcr:InMeshing`, `xcr:Position`). EXIF is consumed only for resolution, focal length, sensor size, make/model, and GPS. So even if bag-extracted frames somehow carried EXIF DateTimeOriginal, RealityScan has no code path to read or re-emit it. For PNGs (normally EXIF-less) there is simply nothing.

Practical implication: to keep a per-frame time reference through RealityScan you must encode it yourself — e.g., name each extracted frame with its ROS `header.stamp` in nanoseconds, because the filename survives into `imageName`/`imagePath` report variables and every CSV/XMP export. That is the only way RealityScan's own outputs can be joined back to your timing analysis. (RealityScan can import a "Trajectory"/flight-log CSV of per-camera positions+orientations, and community CLI tooling shows people baking timestamps into filenames — `cam*_TIMESTAMP.jpg` → `TIMESTAMP_cam*.jpg` — precisely because RealityScan won't carry the time otherwise.)

**Diagnostics that matter for a partial/failed alignment.** Number of components and cameras aligned per component (ideal = all cameras in one component); mean/median/max reprojection error; per-camera reprojection-error distribution and image coverage; tie-point track lengths; and `ExportMisalignmentCameras` connection strengths to localize where alignment broke. If a bad τ or motion blur corrupted the capture, symptoms are: many unaligned cameras, split components, short track lengths, and elevated reprojection error concentrated on the fast-motion frames. The Quality Analysis panel (click a component in the 1Ds view) and the Registration/Component reports surface these; the docs explicitly point to "cameras' and points' count," the Alignment report values, and per-component camera counts as the first-line quality signals.

### ROS2 timing mechanics for this specific rig

Topics: `/image_raw` (Arducam ~28 fps), `/unilidar/cloud` (~12 Hz), `/aft_mapped_to_init` (Point-LIO odometry), `/unilidar/imu`. The Unitree L2's built-in IMU (reporting quaternion, angular_velocity x/y/z, and linear_acceleration x/y/z per the SDK example) and the point cloud share the LiDAR's clock; the camera runs on the host clock via its USB driver. So the LiDAR-inertial subsystem is internally consistent (and Point-LIO further requires per-point `time`), while the camera is the un-synchronized element. Point-LIO itself outputs at very high rate (the paper reports 4–8 kHz odometry, >150 Hz bandwidth), so `/aft_mapped_to_init` gives you a dense, high-rate orientation signal to differentiate into ω for calibration.

**Three clocks, one truth.** Bag record time is `ros2 bag` receive time — subject to USB and scheduling jitter. `header.stamp` on `/image_raw` is written by the camera driver; for UVC/`usb_cam`-style drivers this is typically the time the frame buffer was handed to the driver, i.e., after exposure and USB transfer, and it is known to be jittery and latency-prone. Measured USB-camera end-to-end latencies of roughly 100–300 ms are common on constrained hosts (a Jetson TX1 UVC benchmark showed ~105–168 ms average, and a See3CAM measured a 9-frame ~300 ms latency), and the `usb_cam` driver at low fps has exhibited ~1 s lag tied to a fixed 4-buffer queue. The `libuvc_camera` driver was proposed specifically to give "more accurate timestamps; lower, more predictable latency." The true photon-capture time is earlier than the stamp by that pipeline latency. This is the mechanism behind your ~150–200 ms suspicion — it is real, it lives between (b) `header.stamp` and (c) true capture, and no timestamp field records it.

### Concrete post-mortem recipe

1. **Extract per-message timestamps for all four topics.** Use `rosbag2_py` / the `ros2 bag` API, or `ros2 topic echo --field header.stamp`, or convert to CSV. For each message record both the bag receive time and `header.stamp`; the difference is a per-topic latency proxy. `ros2 bag info` gives per-topic counts and duration for a sanity cross-check.
2. **Check nominal rates and gaps.** Confirm ~28 Hz on `/image_raw`, ~12 Hz on `/unilidar/cloud`, the IMU rate, and Point-LIO odometry rate; histogram inter-message intervals to find dropped frames, buffering stalls, or clock jumps.
3. **First-order offset from stamps.** For each camera frame, find the nearest LiDAR/odometry stamp and look at the residual distribution; a consistent bias is a *lower bound* on τ but not τ itself (it omits the unrecorded camera pipeline latency). `message_filters` `ApproximateTime` with a chosen `slop` is the standard way to pair unsynchronized topics by `header.stamp`, but note it matches on stamps, not true capture time.
4. **Measure τ by motion cross-correlation.** Record a dedicated "clapperboard" sequence: keep the scene fixed and shake the rig with sharp yaw/pitch reversals for 20–30 s. Build ω_lidar(t) from `/aft_mapped_to_init` (differentiate orientation) or directly from `/unilidar/imu` gyro; build ω_cam(t) from optical flow or frame-to-frame essential-matrix rotation on the extracted images. Resample both to a common rate, cross-correlate, take the lag at the correlation peak as τ, and refine with a quadratic fit around the peak (or phase-congruency/CCA if the offset is large).
5. **Decide.** For photogrammetric *fusion* (projecting LiDAR onto images, or coloring points), the tolerable τ depends on rig speed: at a hand-held rotation of ~30°/s, 100 ms of τ smears projection by ~3°; for static or slow captures even 150–200 ms may be visually acceptable, but for any dynamic capture you should correct by shifting camera stamps by τ (time_camera_corrected = time_camera − τ) before association. Validate by re-projecting LiDAR edges onto image edges and checking they line up.

**What RealityScan adds to the timing post-mortem:** it cannot measure τ, but after you fuse, its per-camera reprojection error and component integrity tell you whether the *chosen* τ (and the resulting pose association) produced a coherent reconstruction — a good independent check that your temporal correction was right. If you feed RealityScan poses/priors derived from LiDAR odometry at the wrong τ, the tell-tale is elevated reprojection error and split components on the fast-motion frames.

## Recommendations

1. **Immediately fix the data-provenance gap:** rename every extracted frame to its ROS `header.stamp` in nanoseconds (e.g., `1730724860_502892000.png`). This is the single highest-value step — it makes RealityScan's `imageName` field carry the timing, so poses/errors can be joined to the bag. Do this before any further processing.
2. **Record a dedicated temporal-calibration bag:** 20–30 s of deliberately jerky, multi-axis rotation in a feature-rich scene, camera and LiDAR both running. This is your "sync clapper." Avoid slow smooth pans and blank walls — they make the correlation peak ambiguous.
3. **Estimate τ via IMU-bridged cross-correlation:** compute ω from `/unilidar/imu` (or differentiate `/aft_mapped_to_init`) and ω from camera optical flow, cross-correlate, and take the peak lag. Cross-check by running Kalibr `kalibr_calibrate_imu_camera --time-calibration` (which uses the L2 IMU) to get an independent camera↔IMU `time_offset`; optionally validate the LiDAR↔IMU leg with LI-Init.
4. **Apply and verify:** shift camera timestamps by τ, re-associate frames to LiDAR scans/poses, and confirm with LiDAR-to-image edge reprojection and with RealityScan reprojection-error/component metrics. For spatial extrinsics (a separate step), koide3's `direct_visual_lidar_calibration` can run once the streams are time-aligned.
5. **Thresholds that change the plan:** if the cross-correlation peak is sharp and τ is stable across multiple bags (spread < ~1 frame ≈ 35 ms at 28 fps), a single constant τ correction is sufficient. If τ drifts between bags or the peak is broad/ambiguous, the two clocks are drifting relative to each other and you need either hardware triggering/PTP or per-segment re-estimation. If your capture is essentially static (survey-style), τ is moot and you can skip correction.

## Caveats
- **Firm negative on RealityScan timestamps**, sourced to Epic's own documentation and corroborated by real XMP/CSV exports; if a future RealityScan release adds a time field this would change, but nothing in the 2.1.1/2.2 docs indicates it.
- **The ~150–200 ms figure is a plausible but unverified prior**; it must be re-measured, and it may not be a single constant if the camera driver latency varies with fps or CPU load (documented behavior for `usb_cam`, whose latency jumped to ~1 s at low fps).
- **Cross-correlation can pick a wrong optimum for large offsets** (Mair et al. reported 360 ms mean error in a long-delay case); 200 ms at 28 fps is ~5–6 frames, within the safe range, but verify the peak is unambiguous and refine with quadratic-peak interpolation or phase congruency.
- **No fully turnkey ROS2 camera-LiDAR *temporal* package** was found; the IMU-bridge approach (Kalibr + LI-Init) or a custom cross-correlation script is the practical path. `direct_visual_lidar_calibration` solves spatial extrinsics only and assumes prior time sync.
- **Per-point LiDAR `time` enables de-skew but not camera sync**; it fixes intra-scan motion distortion, a separate problem from the inter-sensor τ. Confirm your `/unilidar/cloud` actually carries the per-point `time` field (some Livox/converted formats collapse all points to one stamp), since Point-LIO and any de-skew depend on it.
- **Rate/offset numbers for your rig (28 fps, 12 Hz, ~150–200 ms)** are your reported values; the recipe re-derives them from the bag rather than trusting the priors.
